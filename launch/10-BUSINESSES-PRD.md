# PRD: Empire, player-owned businesses

**Status:** draft for the owner's review (Nov 4, 2026, version 2). Nothing is built or live. This replaces the first draft (shifts and safe robberies).
**Owner's brief:** "Build all the shops, for sale, around the game." Build it locally, test it, and launch it all together when it's ready.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

**A business is a place you play, not a bank account you defend.** Every business you buy gives you:
- its own job, which pays better than normal work;
- customers, including other players, who pay you;
- a building you can watch grow;
- a kind of risk that fits the business.

## 2. Why

- **Today:** once a player owns the cars and the home they want, cash has nowhere to go and there's no long-term goal.
- **Retention:** businesses give players a reason to log in every day, a goal for weeks (Empire rank), and visible progress.
- **What the game already does well:** businesses lean on driving (deliveries, tours, laundering), crime and cops (shady fronts, raids), crews (working together) and day and night (the nightclub).
- **Why not player-vs-player safe robberies:** with 4–6 players per server, the owner is usually across town, offline, or busy. Those robberies feel bad far more often than they feel fun. Player-vs-player happens only where players **choose** it: shady businesses and police raids.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
1. Five businesses for sale around the city at launch, each with its own job.
2. An owner who plays clearly earns more than a non-owner, but businesses never replace heists and races.
3. Other players spend real money at your business. There's an economy **between** players.
4. Visible growth: every upgrade changes the building.
5. Empire rank: a long-term progression of its own.
6. It works on phone and PC, and everything is config-driven.

**Non-goals (for launch)**
- Earning while offline.
- Selling businesses to other players.
- Robbing other players' wallets.
- Robux-only businesses.
- Hiring NPC staff.

## 5. The three pillars

### Pillar 1: Work there (most of the money)
Each business has a job only owners can do (and their crew; see section 8). It pays about **1.3–1.5×** a normal job like taxi or courier. That's the main way a business makes you money: **owners who play earn more.**

**Car Wash**
- **Wash bay:** a customer car (an NPC car) pulls into a bay. You run a 20–30 s mini-game:
  - **spray** (hold), **scrub** (tap or click the dirty spots), **rinse**;
  - a clean finish pays a tip.
  - Bays and the queue grow with the level.
- **Detail delivery:** drive a freshly washed customer car to their home. **You get to drive their car**, sometimes a sports car. Deliver it fast and without damage for the full tip. Every dent costs you.

**Coffee Shop**
- **Rush orders:** grab an order at the counter and deliver it to 1–3 addresses in sequence against a timer. Faster means a bigger tip.
- Higher levels bring bigger multi-drop orders and "VIP orders" to the mansions and penthouses.

**Print Shop (shady)**
- **Print:** run the presses. Load paper, set the plates, run the press: three short hold stations, about 30 s. That makes **dirty cash** that stacks up in the shop.
- **Launder:**
  1. Load the bags into your car and drive to a random **drop point** across town.
  2. On each run there's a chance a patrol **spots you** (1–2 stars).
  3. Deliver to turn dirty cash into clean cash.
  4. You can only carry so much per run, so more stock means more runs.
- **The catch:** while dirty cash sits in the shop, it can be **raided** (Pillar 3).

**Boat Rentals**
- **Boat tours:** tourists board your boat and you drive a sightseeing route of buoys and landmarks on the water. Smooth driving and hitting the landmarks pay a bonus; crashing scares the tourists (lower tip). Higher levels bring bigger boats and longer, better-paying tours.

**Nightclub**
- **VIP pickups:** drive the club's **limo** (unlocked with the club) to a celebrity at a mansion, hotel or penthouse and bring them to the club. Sometimes **paparazzi** follow you; lose them for a bonus.
- **At night** the club earns double and VIP pickups pay more.

**Every business also earns a small trickle.** A register fills slowly while you're online, whether or not you're working. Collect it at the business, or from the phone at Empire 5. It's never the main money, but the business always feels alive.

### Pillar 2: Watch it grow
Each business has **5 levels**. Every level raises the trickle, the register's size and the job pay, and **changes the building**:

| Level | Car Wash | Coffee | Print Shop | Boat Rentals | Nightclub |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One hand-wash bay, painted sign | A counter and 2 tables | One old press | A dock and one small boat | A small bar, house music |
| 2 | Second bay + a vacuum station | Terrace with umbrellas | Second press, a paper store | Second boat, ticket booth | Dance floor lights |
| 3 | Neon sign, waiting bench, more customers | Drive-through window | Drying racks, a hidden back door | Jet skis for rent | DJ booth + a light show |
| 4 | Detailing garage | Roastery corner, queue outside | Counting room, armored door | Tour boat (bigger tours) | VIP balcony |
| 5 | Full drive-through tunnel with lights | Rooftop café | A full counterfeit floor | Luxury yacht tours | Rooftop pool party |

**More customers appear as a business levels up:** NPC cars at the wash, a queue at the café, people on the dock and the dance floor.

**Empire rank (1–10), a new progression of its own.**
- **Earned by:** finishing business jobs, serving player customers, upgrading, stopping break-ins and surviving raids.
- **Unlocks:**
  - Empire 2: Boat Rentals.
  - Empire 3: the Nightclub.
  - Empire 5: collect your registers from the phone.
  - Empire 7: the "Mogul" title.
  - Empire 10: a gold business sign and the "Kingpin of the Coast" title.
- It shows on the phone's new **EMPIRE** app and next to your rank.

### Pillar 3: Protect it (risk that fits the business)

**Legit businesses can't be robbed by players.** You never come back to find your money gone.
- **NPC break-ins.** Now and then, while you're online and the register holds enough, robbers hit your business (a PvE mini-mission):
  - "Someone's breaking into your Car Wash!" with a 2-minute timer and a GPS route.
  - Get there and scare them off (point a gun, like with pedestrians) or knock them down, for a reward and Empire XP.
  - Miss it and they take up to **10% of the register**. That's all.
  - It never happens more than once per 20 minutes of play, and never in your first 10 minutes.

**Shady businesses are where player-vs-player happens.** Owners choose it by buying one.
- **Police raids (Print Shop, and the Nightclub's back room):**
  - When a shady business holds a lot of dirty cash, **player cops on duty get a raid warrant** and a GPS route.
  - The cops break the door (a hold), search the presses, and **seize the dirty cash**.
  - The owner gets a warning ("Police are on their way to your Print Shop"), plus their crew:
    - get there and **move the stock out** (a laundering run under pressure), or
    - **hold the door** until the warrant runs out (3 minutes).
  - A successful raid pays the cops a reward and costs the owner the dirty cash only. Clean money and upgrades are never touched.
  - **No player cops online?** The AI police raid instead, more slowly and more weakly.
  - This gives player cops a big new job, and keeps the shady business the most profitable and the most dangerous.

## 6. Other players are your customers (money between players)

Players can buy small, useful things at any business. The **owner featured on the sign gets 70%**, and the other 30% is a money sink that keeps the economy healthy.

| Business | Players can buy | Price | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Car Wash | **Drive-through shine** | $100 | Your car sparkles clean for 30 min (a visual effect) |
| Coffee | **Cold brew** | $60 | +25% sprint stamina for 5 min (LocomotionClient's stamina) |
| Boat Rentals | **Jet ski rental** | $150 | Ride a jet ski for 10 min, no boat needed |
| Nightclub | **Cover charge** | $50 | Get into the club: dance floor, music, a social hangout |
| Nightclub | **VIP booth** | $250 | A private booth for you and your crew, for 15 min |
| Print Shop | — | — | No public services; it's a crime front |

**If no owner of that business is in the server,** the purchase still works and the money goes to the city.

## 7. Many owners, one building (how it works)

Like homes, **many players can own the same business**. Each owner has their **own** level, register, stock and jobs. The shared building in the street shows:
- **To an owner:** their **own** level, drawn on their device only.
- **To everyone else:** the level of the **highest-level owner in the server**, with that owner's name on the sign ("Player1's Suds & Shine"). That's the featured owner, who gets the customer money in section 6.
- **To each owner's crew:** the crew owner's level.

The upgrade parts of each level are tagged and switched on per device, the same way night neon is lit per device today. It costs very little.

## 8. Crews

- **Work together.** Crewmates can do your business's jobs at your business. They get their normal pay, and **you get a 20% cut** on top (the game pays it, not them).
- **Busy shop bonus:** while one or more crewmates are at your business, its trickle earns **+25%**.
- **Defend together:** crewmates get your break-in and raid alerts.
- **Phase 2:** a crew co-owner slot.

## 9. Money (targets, tuned in testing)

| | Car Wash | Coffee | Print Shop | Boat Rentals | Nightclub |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | $25k | $45k | $90k | $120k | $250k |
| Trickle at Lv 1 → Lv 5 ($/min online) | 60 → 150 | 96 → 240 | 192 → 450 | 256 → 590 | 375 → 875 by day, ×2 at night |
| Register size Lv 1 → Lv 5 | $2k → $9k | $3.6k → $14.4k | $7k → $26k | $9.6k → $35.4k | $20k → $75k |
| Trickle-only payback at Lv 1 | ~6.9 h | ~7.8 h | ~7.8 h | ~7.8 h | ~7.8 h (over a day and night) |
| Job pay ($/min of work) | ~$1,150 | ~$1,250 | ~$1,600 (with the risk) | ~$1,400 | ~$1,500 |
| Upgrade costs Lv 2–5 | 40% / 60% / 80% / 100% of the price, each level | | | | |

- **The baseline:** active play for a mid-game player is about **$900/min**. Taxi and courier pay $800–1,000/min, and tier-2 heists about $1,000/min.
- **Payback** of the purchase price:
  - trickle alone: about **6–8 hours** online;
  - working the job a fair share of the time: about **2–3 hours**;
  - upgrades pay back at about the same rate.
- **One knob:** `Businesses.RateScale` turns every income up or down at once if the economy runs hot.
- **Nov 4 raise:** Coffee and the Print Shop trickled too slowly for the 6–8 h target (~9.4 h and ~13.6 h). Their trickle and register were raised ×1.2 and ×~1.75 at every level, so both now pay back in ~7.8 h and the registers still take 36–60 minutes to fill. Boat Rentals and the Nightclub get the same treatment when they're built.
- **The Nightclub's night (M5):** the game's day is 14 minutes, and it's night (the neon on) 42.4% of it. The club's first numbers ($180 → $420, ×2 at night) averaged ~$256 a minute and paid back in ~16 h. Given the same treatment (×~2.08), it averages ~$534 a minute at level 1 over a day and night and pays back in **~7.8 h**. Its registers fill in ~37 minutes (level 1) to ~60 (level 5) on that average: slower by day, twice as fast at night.
- **Job pay includes the bonuses:** streaks, PERFECTs, medals, special customers, rush hour and perfect runs are inside each job's $/min above, not on top (`launch/18-BUSINESS-FUN-PASS.md`).
- **Stacking:** owning all five trickles about **$2,305/min** at Lv 5 by day and **$3,180/min** at night (it was $1,330 before the Nov 4 raise, $1,560 before M4 and $1,850 with the Nightclub's first numbers). That's a big late-game reward for $530k of businesses plus ~$1.48M of upgrades, and RateScale is there if it's too much.

## 11. Systems (how it's built)

- **Config:** `Config/Businesses.lua` holds every business, level, price, rate, job setting and the RateScale. A draft exists and will be extended.
- **Server:**
  - `BusinessService`: ownership, levels, registers, customer purchases, Empire XP, break-ins and raids.
  - One job service per business, or one `BusinessJobService` with a section per job, reusing JobService's checked-delivery flow (speed checks, kerb drop spots, END SHIFT).
  - Cash only through EconomyService, Heat through HeatService, rewards through ProgressService.
- **Client:**
  - `BusinessClient`: cards, the EMPIRE app tab, the level props per device, alerts.
  - Mini-game code for the wash bay and the presses.
- **Saved:** `data.businesses[id] = { level, register, dirty, lifetime, jobsDone }`, plus `data.empireXP`. Old saves without these load fine.
- **Security:**
  - The server times every hold.
  - It checks every delivery against the fastest car's speed (as JobService does).
  - Mini-games are checked on the server: a wash can't finish faster than a human could, and auto-clickers are capped.
  - Every remote is rate-limited.
- **Phones:**
  - Business buildings follow the city's Look 3.0 style and stay light (no new big textures).
  - Level props stream with the city.
  - The nightclub interior is built the same way as home interiors (off-map, per visit) so it doesn't load for everyone.

## 12. Success metrics (the first 2 weeks after launch)

- **Adoption:** 30% of returning players buy a business within 3 days.
- **Engagement:** business jobs as a share of all jobs; average Empire rank; upgrades bought per owner.
- **Player economy:** customer purchases per hour per server (target 5+ in busy servers).
- **Balance:**
  - business income as a share of all income (target 30–45% for owners);
  - total cash created per player-hour (watch for inflation);
  - raids per day and the raid success rate (target 40–60%).
- **Retention:** D1 and D7 for owners vs non-owners; session length.

## 13. Risks

| Risk | How we handle it |
|---|---|
| **Scope:** five businesses with five jobs is a big build | Build in milestones (section 14) and keep it all local until everything works. Every job reuses an existing flow (deliveries, tours on the race-checkpoint system, holds). |
| Inflation | Jobs pay only a premium over normal work; RateScale; the 30% sink on customer purchases |
| Many owners confused about whose shop is whose | The featured-owner sign plus your own level drawn just for you (section 7) |
| Auto-clickers or exploits in the mini-games | Server timing, minimum times, rate limits |
| Phone performance with more buildings | Light builds, streamed props, interiors off-map |
| Saving can't be tested in Studio (API access is off) | A private live test before launch, or turn on Studio API access (owner, Creator Hub) |
| Player cops rarely online for raids | AI police raid as a fallback |

3. drying racks and a hidden back door;
4. a counting room and an armored door (breaching takes 9 s instead of 6);
5. a full counterfeit floor.

**The job: one shift with PRINT and LAUNDER** (about $1,600 a minute of work at level 1, risk included). The owner asked for jobs that are fun and satisfying, so:
- **PRINT is a timing game.**
  - **The holds:** three stations in order. Each is a hold with a gauge and a green band.
  - **The grades:** let go in the band for PERFECT, anywhere after the minimum for OK. The server times and grades every hold.
  - **The rewards:** PERFECT prints more bills, and PERFECTs in a row build a streak (×1.05 up to ×1.3, shown on the strip).
  - **The feel:** a press "ka-chunk", sheets fluttering out, a "+$X DIRTY" pop, and a stack of fake bills on the counting table that grows on the owner's device.
  - **The stock:** dirty cash is saved per owner (`dirty`). It isn't spendable, and its cap grows with the level.
- **LAUNDER is a tense drive.**
  - **Loading:** park your own car in the loading bay and hold LOAD at the cash cart.
  - **The drive:** take the bags to a drop 800–1,600 studs away (GPS and ring).
  - **The heat meter:** shown on the strip, it fills near police (player cops, AI patrols, officers, the police HQ) and drains when clear. Full means spotted (1–2 stars). Small random tip-offs on top.
  - **Wanted:** it doesn't end the run.
  - **Lost:** an arrest, death, leaving the car or the car going loses the bags.
  - **The drop:** the clean cash counts up on screen with a register, then the LAUNDERED stamp. A clean run (never spotted) pays +10%.
  - **Bonuses:** the streak and the clean-run bonus are part of the ~$1,600 average, not on top of it.
- **The shift summary** when it ends: press runs, PERFECTs, best streak, runs, clean runs, $ printed and earned.
- **Crewmates** can print into the owner's stock and launder it (20% cut to the owner).

**Police raids** (pillar 3): a warrant can come while the owner's dirty cash is $5,500+.
- **When:** never in their first 10 minutes, at most every 15 minutes, one per building, likelier the fuller the stock.
- **Player cops on duty** (not the owner's crew) get the dispatch.
  - They hold BREACH at the door, then SEARCH at the presses, to seize all the dirty cash.
  - They're paid $300 each plus 25% of it (a SEIZED $X stamp).
- **No cop on duty:** an AI raid instead. A police car pulls up and two officers breach and search more slowly, only while nobody holds the door. It takes half the stock.
- **The owner and crew** get the warning: a toast, a siren, the X flashing red, the 3-minute countdown. They can:
  - launder stock out;
  - fight (the normal combat rules);
  - or hold the door: stay inside. If the warrant runs out with no seizure, it's RAID FAILED: $1,000 and Empire XP for the owner, and $500 and XP for each crewmate who held it with them.
- **What's at stake:** only the dirty cash. Clean money, the register and upgrades are never touched.
- **NPC break-ins** never pick a shady business. Collecting the register gives no star (the M3 notes: raids, not collect stars).

**Also:** the map (a violet **X**, a gold ring when it's yours, flashing red in a raid), the EMPIRE app (dirty cash and the raid clock), the manage card (dirty cash, what a run carries, the raid), and the Studio helpers (`dirty`, `raid`, `raid ai`, `endraid`, `testcop`, `heat`).

**One shared file changed:** `PhoneUI.lua` (two lines: the EMPIRE app shows the business's own line when BusinessClient gives one).
